# Lesson 2 — Loud, Soft, Fast, Slow

**Unit 1: Explore** · 30 minutes

**You need:** piano, soft ball, rhythm sticks or two spoons, color cards,
red C sticker.

## I can…

- echo loud and soft sounds;
- echo fast and slow sounds;
- keep a steady beat for eight counts;
- play red C with a gentle touch.

## At a glance

| Min | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Hello song with claps |
| 3–7 | Marching and freezing |
| 7–12 | Loud/soft review game |
| 12–19 | Steady beat and the conductor game |
| 19–26 | Piano echoes on red C |
| 26–28 | Child conducts the teacher |
| 28–30 | Home card |

## 1. Hello song (3 min)

"Hel-lo, [name], hel-lo!" on G–E. This week add a stomp on "hel" and a clap on
"lo." Keep the same speed each time.

## 2. March and freeze (4 min)

March around the room to a steady count: "1, 2, 3, 4." On "freeze," stop
completely. Repeat with the teacher clapping instead of counting. Then clap
the beat while marching. The body learns the pulse before the hands do.

## 3. Loud and soft (5 min)

Review high/low with the stuffed animal. Then add dynamics:

- **Bear voice:** low and loud (but not shouted).
- **Mouse voice:** high and soft.
- **Sleeping baby:** very soft.

Child chooses an animal, teacher plays, child guesses. Then swap.

## 4. Steady beat and the conductor (7 min)

- Tap the soft ball on the floor on each count: "1, 2, 3, 4."
- Child taps, teacher counts. Keep it slow enough to stay together.
- **Conductor game:** the child holds a stick like a baton. Fast conducting
  means fast clapping; a low baton means soft; a high baton means loud. The
  teacher follows. This gives the child control and teaches the vocabulary
  from the giving end.

## 5. Piano echoes (7 min)

All playing stays on red C or on the black keys so the hand does not need to
move.

1. Teacher plays four beats on red C. Child echoes four beats.
2. Teacher plays loud, child echoes loud. Then soft.
3. Teacher plays fast, child echoes fast. Then slow.
4. Teacher plays a pattern: loud-loud-soft-soft. Child echoes.

Say the words while playing: "loud, loud, soft, soft." Do not use the Italian
terms yet.

## 6. Child conducts (2 min)

The child conducts the teacher at the piano: fast, slow, loud, soft, and
"sleeping." End with a dramatic silence and a bow.

## 7. Home card (2 min)

> **Practice card 2 — 5 minutes, 5 days**
>
> 1. March and clap 1-2-3-4.
> 2. Play red C four times: loud, soft, loud, soft.
> 3. Make a bear sound and a mouse sound.
> 4. Teach the conductor game to a grown-up.
>
> Grown-up: let the child be in charge. Follow their baton.

## Teacher notes

- "Loud" at this age can become banging. Demonstrate a full-arm weight drop
  onto the key rather than a finger poke; loud should still be beautiful.
- If the child cannot keep a steady beat, tap on their back or shoulders while
  they clap. The feeling of the pulse often arrives before the control.
- Keep the tempo slow. Speed hides errors.

## If the child races ahead

Ask for a four-beat pattern that is loud-soft-soft-loud. Echo it. Add a rest
("shh") and see if the child can keep the beat through the silence.

## If the child struggles

Reduce to two sounds: loud and soft only. Use one key. Success is one clean
echo.
