# Lesson 6 — Walk and Run-ning

**Unit 2: Colors Make Music** · 30 minutes

**You need:** piano, rhythm sticks, Hot Cross Buns chart, Mary Had a Little
Lamb chart (printables page 9), blank paper.

## I can…

- hear one sound and two sounds in a beat;
- clap the rhythm of "Mary had a little lamb";
- play the first phrase of *Mary Had a Little Lamb*;
- keep the beat while I play.

## At a glance

| Min | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Hello song |
| 3–7 | Rhythm echo warm-up |
| 7–12 | *Hot Cross Buns* review |
| 12–19 | Walk, run-ning, and Mary's words |
| 19–26 | Phrase 1 at the piano |
| 26–28 | Tempo conductor |
| 28–30 | Home card |

## 1. Hello song (3 min)

Sing the hello song. Clap the rhythm of the child's name: "E-va" (run-ning) or
"Sa-mu-el" (two walks and a run-ning). Child copies.

## 2. Rhythm echo (4 min)

Teacher claps a four-beat pattern using walk and run-ning. Child echoes.
Start with two patterns, then three. Keep the pulse in the feet.

```
walk  walk  run-ning  walk
run-ning  walk  walk  shh
walk  run-ning  run-ning  walk
```

## 3. Hot Cross Buns review (5 min)

Play the song from the chart. Then play it again with one change:

- very soft;
- a little faster but even;
- with a "sleepy" ending.

Check the rest: count it aloud. If the child rushes the C C C C / D D D D
section, clap the words first.

## 4. Mary's rhythm (7 min)

Say the words and clap them naturally:

> "Ma-ry had a lit-tle lamb, lit-tle lamb, lit-tle lamb."

Ask: "Which words have two claps?" (Ma-ry, lit-tle.) "Which have one?"
(Mary, had, a, lamb.)

Show that one beat can hold two sounds: **run-ning**. One beat with one sound
is **walk**. A silent beat is **shh**.

Map the first phrase on the chart with a pencil: circle the two-sound beats.
Do not write note values yet. The ear and the body lead; the symbol follows.

## 5. Phrase 1 at the piano (7 min)

The first phrase is E D C D E E E. Teach it in two pieces:

1. **E D C D** — "Ma-ry had a" — fingers 3 2 1 2.
2. **E E E** — "lit-tle lamb" — finger 3, three times.

Then join them. Play slowly with a beat:

```
E  D  C  D  E  E  E
1  2  3  4  1  2  3
```

The last three E's can be even walks, even though the words are quick. At this
stage, even is more important than exact.

Play phrase 1 three times. Then stop and sing it. Then play it once more with
the teacher clapping the beat.

## 6. Tempo conductor (2 min)

Child conducts *Hot Cross Buns* or phrase 1 of Mary at three speeds: sleepy,
walking, running. The teacher plays and the child keeps the beat with a stick.
Swap.

## 7. Home card (2 min)

> **Practice card 6 — 5 minutes, 5 days**
>
> 1. Clap: walk, walk, run-ning, walk.
> 2. Say "Ma-ry had a lit-tle lamb" and clap the words.
> 3. Play Mary phrase 1: E D C D E E E, three times.
> 4. Play *Hot Cross Buns* once for an audience.
>
> Grown-up: clap the beat while the child plays. Do not clap the rhythm.

## Teacher notes

- "Run-ning" is two sounds in one beat, not necessarily a notation lesson.
  Keep the language physical.
- The E E E ending often becomes a race. Put a hand on the child's shoulder
  and count "1, 2, 3" slowly.
- If the child sings "lit-tle lamb" with a jump, let it be. The E–G skip comes
  in Lesson 7.

## If the child races ahead

Teach phrase 2 (D D D, then E G G). Point out that the hand jumps from yellow
E to blue G. Practise just that jump five times.

## If the child struggles

Keep only E D C D. That is still phrase 1's first half. Add E E E next week.
For rhythm, clap only walk patterns and leave run-ning for later.
