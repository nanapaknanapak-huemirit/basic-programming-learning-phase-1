# Lesson 7 — Mary Had a Little Lamb

**Unit 2: Colors Make Music** · 30 minutes

**You need:** piano, Mary chart (printables page 9), three colored markers or
stickers, a scarf.

## I can…

- play the first phrase from memory;
- leap from yellow E to blue G;
- play the whole of *Mary Had a Little Lamb*;
- keep a curved hand for eight notes.

## At a glance

| Min | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Hello song |
| 3–7 | Leap warm-up |
| 7–12 | Phrase 1 review and song map |
| 12–19 | Phrases 2, 3, and 4 |
| 19–26 | Whole song and performance |
| 26–28 | Soft ending game |
| 28–30 | Home card |

## 1. Hello song (3 min)

Sing the hello song. Today, sing it in a "lamb voice": soft and a little
wobbly. Child leads.

## 2. Leap warm-up (4 min)

The only skip in the song is E to G. Make it a game.

- "Owl hoot": play E with finger 3, then G with finger 5. Two long notes.
- "Bunny hop": E–G, E–G, four times.
- Check the hand: thumb stays near red C, pinky stretches to blue G, wrist
  level.
- If the stretch is uncomfortable, let the whole hand slide up one key and
  play G with finger 4. Comfort beats a stretched pinky.

## 3. Phrase 1 and the song map (5 min)

Play phrase 1: E D C D E E E. Then look at the whole chart.

- Put a sticker at the start of each phrase (four phrases).
- Phrase 1 starts yellow E.
- Phrase 2 starts orange D, then leaps to blue G.
- Phrase 3 is phrase 1 with an extra E.
- Phrase 4 walks down to red C.

Let the child color the four phrase starts. This is the "map" of the song.

## 4. Phrases 2, 3, and 4 (7 min)

**Phrase 2:** D D D, then E G G.
- Play D D D with finger 2.
- Pause. Find blue G. Play E G G.
- Join: D D D E G G. The pause is allowed at first.

**Phrase 3:** E D C D E E E E.
- Same as phrase 1, with one extra E at the end. Count the E's: "four little
  lambs."

**Phrase 4:** D D E D C.
- Walk down: D D, up to E, down D, home to C.
- This is the ending. Play it alone five times.

## 5. Whole song (7 min)

Put the phrases together slowly. Point to the phrase starts if needed.

1. Play phrases 1–2.
2. Play phrases 3–4.
3. Play all four.
4. Play again, softer.
5. Perform for the stuffed animal with a bow.

If the child loses the place, stop, sing the words from the beginning, and
restart at the nearest phrase. Do not restart from the very beginning every
time; that teaches the beginning and nothing else.

## 6. Soft ending game (2 min)

The last line is "its fleece was white as snow." Put a scarf on the piano and
play phrase 4 as softly as snow. Then play it "like a muddy puddle" (loud) for
contrast. The child chooses which one is the real ending: snow.

## 7. Home card (2 min)

> **Practice card 7 — 5 minutes, 5 days**
>
> 1. Bunny hops: E–G, four times.
> 2. Phrase 2: D D D, then E G G.
> 3. Play the whole song slowly, once a day.
> 4. Snow ending: phrase 4 very softly.
>
> Grown-up: let the child restart at a phrase, not always at the beginning.

## Teacher notes

- The leap E–G is the main technical hurdle. Keep the thumb anchored and let
  the pinky reach; do not rotate the whole forearm.
- The extra E in phrase 3 is easy to miss. Count "four little lambs" aloud.
- If the child plays from memory and ignores the chart, that is a success, not
  a problem. Ask them to point to the chart while singing afterward.

## If the child races ahead

Add a left-hand red C on beat 1 of each phrase. Or transpose the song to G by
moving the color stickers up: the color pattern stays the same, the letters
change.

## If the child struggles

Keep phrases 1 and 2 only. Make phrase 2 a call-and-response: teacher plays
D D D, child plays E G G. The full song can wait a week.
