# Lesson 10 — Steps and Skips

**Unit 3: Colors Become Notes** · 30 minutes

**You need:** piano, Ode to Joy chart (printables page 11), staff sheet,
Twinkle chart, painter's tape or paper "stones" for the floor.

## I can…

- show a step and a skip on the floor and on the keys;
- find a step and a skip on the staff;
- play the first phrase of *Ode to Joy*;
- keep the beat while the melody moves by step.

## At a glance

| Min | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Hello song |
| 3–7 | Step and skip stones |
| 7–12 | Twinkle and staff review |
| 12–19 | Steps on the keyboard |
| 19–26 | Ode to Joy |
| 26–28 | Step or skip? |
| 28–30 | Home card |

## 1. Hello song (3 min)

Sing the hello song. Child chooses a "step" hello (two neighboring notes) or a
"skip" hello (a jump). Teacher copies.

## 2. Step and skip stones (4 min)

Lay a row of paper stones on the floor.

- **Step:** move to the very next stone.
- **Skip:** jump over one stone.
- Walk four steps, then two skips. Clap on each landing.

Then do it at the keyboard: C–D is a step; C–E is a skip. G–A is a step; G–E
is a skip down.

## 3. Twinkle review (5 min)

Play Twinkle phrase 1. Then point to the staff and ask:

- "C to C: step, skip, or same?"
- "C to G: step, skip, or same?" (skip)
- "A to G: step, skip, or same?" (step)

No need to name intervals. "Skip" and "step" are enough.

## 4. Steps on the keyboard (7 min)

Play slowly and name the movement:

```
C D E F G   up by step
G F E D C   down by step
C E G       up by skip
G E C       down by skip
```

Then a mixed pattern: C D E G E D C. The child says "step, step, skip, skip,
step, step" while playing. This is hard; slow it down and laugh at mistakes.

## 5. Ode to Joy (7 min)

The first phrase is almost all steps. That makes it perfect for this week.

1. Sing it on "da": E E F G, G F E D.
2. Play the first four notes: E E F G (fingers 3 3 4 5).
3. Play the next four: G F E D (5 4 3 2).
4. Join the eight notes. Then add the answer: C C D E, E D D.
5. Play the whole phrase slowly. The last D is held for two beats.

Point out: there are no skips in this phrase. It is all steps and repeated
notes. That is why it sounds smooth.

If the child is ready, add the repeat of the phrase ending on C:
C C D E, D C C.

## 6. Step or skip? (2 min)

Teacher plays two notes. Child says "step" or "skip." Use big, clear
distances. Then the child plays and the teacher guesses. Keep it fast and
silly.

## 7. Home card (2 min)

> **Practice card 10 — 5 minutes, 5 days**
>
> 1. Walk four steps and two skips on the floor.
> 2. Play C D E F G up and back, naming "step."
> 3. Play Ode to Joy, first phrase, twice.
> 4. Play "step or skip?" with a grown-up.
>
> Grown-up: "skip" means one key in between, not a big jump across the piano.

## Teacher notes

- Children often call any larger movement a "skip." Keep the definition
  physical: step = next stone, skip = one stone hidden underneath.
- Ode to Joy's repeated E and G are easy to rush. Count the beat aloud.
- The fingering 3 3 4 5 for E E F G can collapse the pinky. Check the bubble
  before adding speed.

## If the child races ahead

Teach the second half of the phrase (E E F G, G F E D, C C D E, D C C) and
play the whole eight-bar melody. Add a left-hand C drone on beat 1.

## If the child struggles

Play only E E F G. Four notes, one line, slow. Add the answer next week.
