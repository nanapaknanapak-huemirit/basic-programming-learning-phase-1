# Lesson 12 — Recital and Create

**Unit 3: Colors Become Notes** · 30 minutes · Final lesson

**You need:** piano, favorite charts, plain paper cards or the letter chart
(page 6), compose sheet,
certificate (printables page 16), sticker chart, phone for photos, a small
audience if possible.

## I can…

- perform a piece from start to finish;
- read a four-note pattern without color;
- play a melody with a left-hand drone;
- compose and play a short song.

## At a glance

| Min | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Hello song and celebration |
| 3–7 | Rainbow warm-up |
| 7–12 | Plain-note challenge |
| 12–20 | Recital |
| 20–26 | New composition |
| 26–28 | Certificate and photo |
| 28–30 | What comes next |

## 1. Hello song (3 min)

Sing the hello song one last time. Let the child add a drum roll on the closed
lid before the final "hello."

## 2. Rainbow warm-up (4 min)

Play C D E F G up and back, slow and even. Then play it with a left-hand C
drone. Then play "steps and skips": C E G E C. No corrections this week
unless something hurts. This is a celebration of what the hands can do.

## 3. Plain-note challenge (5 min)

Write four letters on a plain card, or point to the letter chart: **E D C D**.
Ask the child to play it.
Then **G F E D**. Then put them together: E D C D, G F E D.

If letters are still shaky, point to the staff notes on page 14 instead. The
goal is to use a cue other than color. Say the letter names aloud together
first.

Then play "mystery song": teacher plays a four-note pattern without color and
the child names or plays it back.

## 4. Recital (8 min)

Set up the audience. The child chooses one or two pieces from:

- *Hot Cross Buns*
- *Mary Had a Little Lamb*
- *Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star* (one or two phrases)
- *Ode to Joy*
- *Jingle Bells*
- their own composition

Routine:

1. Walk to the piano.
2. Bow.
3. Sit, hands in lap, breathe.
4. Play.
5. Bow. Wait for applause.

If the child stops in the middle, smile and say "start again from the
beginning" or "start from the blue part." The performance is practice for
being brave, not a test.

## 5. New composition (6 min)

Compose a "goodbye song" using four to eight boxes on the compose sheet.

- Use at least one plain (no-color) note.
- Use at least one color note.
- End on red C so it sounds finished.
- Add a left-hand C drone if the child wants.

Play it twice. The second time can be a "grand finale" with a held C in both
hands.

## 6. Certificate and photo (2 min)

Sign the certificate. Let the child put the final sticker on the progress
chart. Take a photo at the piano with the certificate. Applaud again.

## 7. What comes next (2 min)

> **Practice card 12 — keep playing**
>
> 1. Play your favorite three songs each week.
> 2. Keep the plain-note cards nearby.
> 3. Practise five minutes a day, or whenever the piano looks fun.
> 4. Choose one new song to learn next.

Tell the child concretely what they can now do: "You can find seven keys, read
colors and letters, keep a beat, play with two hands, and perform for people."
That sentence is the real certificate.

## Teacher notes

- Invite the audience for the last ten minutes only, not the whole lesson.
- Keep the recital optional if the child is anxious. A performance for one
  stuffed animal counts.
- Hand over the printed charts and the parent guide. Recommend a beginner
  method book that uses letter names, with the color stickers kept as a
  reference for another month.
- Record the child's favorite piece for the family. Ask permission first.

## After the recital

Next goals:

- read steps and skips without color;
- hands together in parallel motion;
- five-finger scales in C, G, and F;
- dynamics and legato/staccato;
- one new short piece every two weeks.

Keep the routine warm and short. The child who associates the piano with
feeling capable will keep coming back.
