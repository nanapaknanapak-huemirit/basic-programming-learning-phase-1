# Parent and Carer Guide

## The short version

Five minutes a day, five days a week. Sit nearby. Praise the try, not just the
result. Stop while it is still fun.

## Why so short?

A 4–6-year-old's attention for a focused physical skill is roughly 3–8
minutes. Five happy minutes repeated daily builds more than one 30-minute
session on Saturday. The goal is a habit, not a marathon.

## A practice session

1. **Warm-up (1 min).** Shake hands, wiggle fingers, make ten soft "raindrop"
   touches on the closed lid.
2. **Review (2 min).** Play the previous week's song twice. The first time is
   allowed to be messy.
3. **New bit (1–2 min).** Practise only the new measure or the new color.
4. **Fun finish (1 min).** Improvise, play a favorite, or bow to an audience
   of stuffed animals.

## The practice card

Each lesson plan ends with a home card. It lists:

- one warm-up,
- one song or pattern,
- one tiny new task,
- one "just for fun" activity.

Do not add to the card. If the child wants more, let them improvise.

## How to help at the piano

- **Point, don't play.** Point to the next color on the chart instead of
  pressing the key for them.
- **Sing the color names.** "Red, red, blue, blue…" Hearing the map helps.
- **Count out loud, slowly.** "1, 2, 3, 4." Slow is a speed, not a failure.
- **One instruction at a time.** "Curved fingers" and "steady beat" together
  is too much.
- **Let mistakes finish.** Stopping on every wrong note teaches the child to
  fear the keyboard.

## What not to do

- Do not compare with siblings, classmates, or videos.
- Do not promise a reward for practising; let the playing be the reward.
- Do not sit on top of the child or hold their hands unless they ask.
- Do not correct posture constantly. Pick one thing per session.

## When the child resists

| Behaviour | Try |
|---|---|
| "I don't want to" | Offer two choices: "Red song or blue song?" |
| Plays silly and loud | Join the silliness for 30 seconds, then "now the sleeping keys." |
| Cries | Stop. Snack, movement, or a hug. Try again tomorrow. |
| Only wants the same song | Let them. Fluency is built by repetition. Add one new note at the end. |
| Won't use the chart | Sing the song and let them copy you by ear. Return to the chart later. |

## Supporting practice without a piano at home

- A paper keyboard from the printables works for finger numbers and color
  pointing.
- Clap the rhythm on the table.
- Sing the color names.
- Tap the melody on a table edge with one finger.

## The recital

Lesson 12 ends with a mini recital. Invite one or two listeners. Keep it
short, clap a lot, and take a photo. Do not video the whole thing from behind
a phone — watch with your eyes.

## Questions to ask the teacher

- Which song is secure enough to perform?
- Which one small thing should we practise this week?
- Is the bench height still right?
- What should we do when practice turns into a battle?

## A note on progress

Children progress in uneven steps, not a straight line. A child may play a
song beautifully on Tuesday and forget it on Thursday. That is normal. The
skills return. Keep the sessions short, warm, and regular, and the piano will
become a place the child feels competent.
